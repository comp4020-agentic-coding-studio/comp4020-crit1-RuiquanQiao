P199 Teletext

About this service

The medium

Teletext carried pages of forty columns by twenty-four rows, in seven colours on black. There was no scrolling and no layout: text either fitted the grid or it was cut. You did not follow a link, you typed three digits and waited for the page to come round again.

Why it looks like this

Ceefax, the BBC's teletext service, ran from 1974 until October 2012. The interface outlived the technology in memory but not on air. These pages put a live news feed back into it.

Where the news comes from

Headlines and summaries are from the BBC News public feed, copyright the British Broadcasting Corporation. This is a student homage, not a BBC service, and nothing here is endorsed by the BBC.

How it is built

The feed is read when the site is built, never in your browser: there is no JavaScript on any page here. The build wraps every line to the grid, so the breaks you see are computed rather than typed. When the feed cannot be reached the build serves a committed snapshot, because a stale page is a teletext page and a failed build is nothing at all.

The clock

The time in the header is when the feed was read, not when you loaded the page. Teletext pages were snapshots too. The source is British but the reader is not, so times are Canberra's, converted.
